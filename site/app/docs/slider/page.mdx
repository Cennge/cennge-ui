## When to use

A Slider suits a value where the position matters more than the exact digits: a threshold, a time window, a level. People can drag it, tap a mark, or step it from the keyboard, and the current value shows next to the label. When people know the exact number they want, a [NumberInput](/docs/number-input/) is quicker.

## Similar

- [NumberInput](/docs/number-input/) is for exact numbers that are typed.
- [Radio](/docs/radio/) shows a handful of named options as a list; a Slider with named marks keeps them on one scale.
- [Progress](/docs/progress/) shows a value but takes no input.

## One value

A number in `value` gives one thumb. `formatValue` sets how the value reads next to the label and to screen readers. `onValueChange` fires on every step of a drag; `onValueCommit` fires once when the drag ends, and on each key press, which suits saving.

## A range

An array gives a thumb per item. The thumbs are named after the label, "Changed lines Minimum" and "Changed lines Maximum"; `thumbLabels` renames or translates the part after the label.

## Marks

Each mark puts a dot on the track; a mark with a `label` also writes it under the track, and a click on that label moves the thumb there. A plain number, like 90 here, is a dot without a label. Dots on the filled part of the track change colour.

## Only on the marks

`restrictToMarks` makes the marks the only values: a drag snaps to the nearest one and the arrow keys go from mark to mark, so steps need not be even.

## Named steps

When every value the slider can take has a text label, the label becomes the value's name: the readout and screen readers say "Warning", not "1".

## Accessibility

Each thumb is a `slider` with its value, minimum and maximum, and a text value from `formatValue` or a mark's label. A single thumb takes its name from `label`, or from `aria-label` when there is no visible label. The labels under the track are hidden from screen readers: they repeat what the thumbs already say, and the keyboard reaches every value without them. A disabled slider ignores clicks on its labels.
