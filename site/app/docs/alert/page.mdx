## When to use

An Alert belongs to a place on the page and says something about it: a failed build above its logs, a key that is about to expire on the settings page. It stays until the page removes it, so it suits a state that lasts, not the result of a click a moment ago.

## Similar

- [Toast](/docs/toast/) reports what happened a moment ago from a corner of the screen, then leaves by itself.
- [AlertDialog](/docs/alert-dialog/) stops everything until the reader answers; an Alert asks nothing.
- [EmptyState](/docs/empty-state/) fills a list that has nothing in it; it is not a message about an event.

## Variants

`flat` is a tint of the colour and the default. `bordered` draws only an edge, `faded` sets a neutral panel with an edge, and `solid` fills the box with the colour for the one message that must not be missed.

## Colours and what screen readers hear

The colour also sets the role. `danger` and `warning` render `role="alert"`, which interrupts a screen reader; every other colour renders `role="status"`, which waits its turn. Success, warning and danger have icons of their own; the other colours share an info mark.

## Closing it, acting on it

`isClosable` adds a close button with the name "Close". The Alert keeps no open state of its own: hide it in `onClose`, as here. `endContent` takes the action that fixes the problem.
