## When to use

Use an Indicator to flag something about one control: unread notifications on a bell, a person's status on their avatar, a new entry on a tab. It sits on the corner without taking space, so the layout around the control does not change when the count appears or goes.

## Similar

- [Badge](/docs/badge/) is a label in the flow of the text, with room for words; an Indicator is pinned on top of another element.
- [Avatar](/docs/avatar/) is often what the Indicator sits on; status dots belong on the avatar, not beside it.

## Counts and dots

`content` takes a number or a short word. Numbers above `max` show as `max+`. Without `content` it is a plain dot, sized by `size`. The colour is `danger` unless you choose another.

## Status on an avatar

On a circle the corner of the box is empty space. `shape="circle"` moves the indicator onto the rim; `placement` picks the corner. Write the status beside the avatar as well, as here, so it does not rest on colour alone.

## Accessibility

An Indicator is decorative until you give it a `label`: then screen readers read the label, "3 unread notifications", instead of a bare "3". While `isInvisible` is on, the label is not read either. Keep the name of the control itself, such as "Notifications", on the control.

## Styling

The ring that parts the indicator from what it sits on is `ring-background`, the page colour. On a panel or a card, pass the panel's colour in `className`, such as `ring-surface-3`, as the bar on the plate does; `showOutline={false}` removes the ring.
