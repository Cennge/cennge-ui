## When to use

An Avatar shows who or what something belongs to: the account in a header, the author of a comment, the owner of a repository. The `name` does two jobs: it is the picture's alt text, and without a picture it becomes the initials, set on one of seven `color` tints.

## Similar

- [Indicator](/docs/indicator/) puts a status dot or a count on the avatar's corner; the two go together.
- [Skeleton](/docs/skeleton/) holds the avatar's place while the account itself is still loading. An Avatar waiting for its picture already shows initials.

## Picture, initials or your own

A single word gives its first two letters; several give the first letter of the first and last. `fallback` replaces the initials with anything, such as an icon for a signed-out visitor.

## Sizes and shapes

Four sizes, from 32 to 64 pixels. Avatars are round by default; `radius="md"` reads as a project or an organisation rather than a person.

## A group with a counter

`AvatarGroup` overlaps its avatars and, past `max`, adds a counter of the rest. `isBordered` draws a ring in the page colour around each avatar so the overlaps stay apart. The group's `size` sets only the counter's size, so give it the same size as the avatars.

</Example>

## With a status

Wrap an avatar in an [Indicator](/docs/indicator/) with `shape="circle"`: the dot moves in from the empty corner onto the edge of the circle. Its `label` is what screen readers hear.

<Example file="avatar/status">
  <Status />
</Example>

## Server rendering

An avatar without `src` is rendered with its initials on the server. One with `src` is rendered empty: the picture is shown only after the browser has loaded it, and the initials only if it has not loaded within 300 ms, so a cached picture does not flash letters first.
