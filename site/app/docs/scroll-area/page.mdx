## When to use

Use a ScrollArea for a part of the page that scrolls on its own: a commit list in a panel, a log, a row of filters. It keeps the browser's own scrolling, with its momentum, wheel and touch, and draws the scrollbars in the theme's colours on every system.

## Similar

- [VirtualList](/docs/virtual-list/) renders only the rows in view; use it for thousands of rows. A ScrollArea renders all of its content.
- [Carousel](/docs/carousel/) snaps slides into place as they scroll sideways, with controls to step through them.

## A list with long lines

Vertical content keeps the width of the box, so `truncate` ends a long line with an ellipsis instead of making the box scroll sideways. `fadeEdges` fades the content out at each edge that still hides some of it; `fadeSize` sets how far the fade reaches.

## Both ways

`orientation="horizontal"` scrolls a row sideways, as the repositories on the plate do; `"both"` lets wide and tall content scroll either way, and a corner joins the two bars. `type` says when the bars show: on `hover` (the default), while scrolling, whenever the content overflows (`auto`), or `always`.

## Accessibility

A box whose content fits is not a tab stop and has no role. Once the content overflows it takes focus from <kbd>Tab</kbd>, so keyboard users can scroll it, and with an `aria-label` it becomes a `region` that screen readers list. Name every ScrollArea that may overflow.

## Styling

`classNames` reaches the parts: `base` (the box), `viewport` (the element that scrolls), `scrollbar` and `thumb`. Give the box its height, border and background with `className`.
