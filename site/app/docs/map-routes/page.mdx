## When to use

Use a MapRoute for a way along points in order: a delivery, a track, a walk. Use a MapArc for pairs of places where the way between them does not matter, only that they are joined: shipments, transfers, flights.

Both draw into the canvas below the map's labels, so street and borough names stay readable over them. Their colour is one of the library's roles, `primary` by default, which follows the accent. Neither is read out: name the places in text or with [markers](/docs/map-markers/), as the examples do.

## Similar

- [MapGeoJSON](/docs/map-data/) draws lines from GeoJSON too, with a tooltip on hover and a colour by value. Use it for many lines that carry data, a MapRoute for one way you draw.
- A [Timeline](/docs/timeline/) lists the stops of a route in order, with times: a good companion for readers who cannot see the map.

## A route through stops

`coordinates` are the line's points in order, as `[longitude, latitude]`. An edge in the background's colour runs under the line, so it reads over any street or park; `hasOutline={false}` drops it.

## Travelled and still to go

New `coordinates` replace the line's data without adding it again, so a line can follow a slider or live positions. Here two routes share the way: the part still to go is thin, dashed and grey, and the part travelled, drawn after it, lies on top.

## Arcs between places

`arcs` takes pairs of points and joins each with a curve. `curvature` sets how far it bows, as a share of its length, `0.2` by default; a negative one bows the other way. A curve bows to the left of its way, so a trip there and one back are two curves, not one. Arcs are 2 px wide and, unlike routes, have no edge under them unless you pass `hasOutline`.

## Accessibility

A line on the canvas is a picture: screen readers do not see it. Put what it shows in text as well: the stops in order, the places joined. Markers at the stops are read out by name, and the map's `aria-label` can say where the route goes.
