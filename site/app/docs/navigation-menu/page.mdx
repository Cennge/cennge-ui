## When to use

A NavigationMenu is the top bar of a site or of its documentation: a few triggers whose panels hold links, and plain links beside them. Everything in it takes the reader to another page, and it fits dozens of links into one row by keeping them in panels until someone asks.

The panel is one element shared by the whole bar, as in Radix, so moving from trigger to trigger swaps its content without closing and reopening. Unlike Radix, it opens under the trigger you are on rather than at the start of the bar, and it moves back inside the window when the trigger sits near an edge.

## Similar

- [Dropdown](/docs/dropdown/) runs actions from a button and is read as a menu of commands; a NavigationMenu holds links and is read as navigation.
- [Sidebar](/docs/sidebar/) keeps an app's links on screen in a column, and in a drawer on phones; a NavigationMenu hides them in panels to save the room.

## Links and the current page

`NavigationMenuLink` with a `title` and a `description` is a two-line link in a panel; alone in a `NavigationMenuItem` it is a link in the bar. With `asChild` it styles your router's link, here Next.js `Link`, and `isActive` highlights the current page and marks it with `aria-current="page"`.

## Open on click

`openOn="click"` stops the resting pointer from opening panels. A click, Enter or Space opens one; the pointer can leave it and it stays open until Escape, a click elsewhere or another click on its trigger.

</Example>

## Vertical

With `orientation="vertical"` the triggers stack, the panel opens beside the trigger, level with it, and the right arrow goes into it. On a screen too narrow for both, the panel moves left over the list instead of off the edge.

## Links in the HTML for search engines

A panel is rendered only while it is open, so a crawler reading the page finds no links in it. `keepMounted` keeps every panel in the page, hidden while closed, so their links are in the HTML from the start.

## Accessibility

Give each NavigationMenu an `aria-label`, so screen readers can tell it from the other navigation on the page. Triggers are buttons that say whether their panel is open; the arrow keys, Home and End move between the triggers and links of the bar, and Tab goes on into an open panel.
