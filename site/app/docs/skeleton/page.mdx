## When to use

Use a Skeleton when you know the shape of what is loading: a repository card, a list of releases, a profile. The reader sees where things will be, and nothing jumps when the data arrives. When the shape is unknown, or the wait is for an action rather than content, a spinner is more honest.

## Similar

- [Spinner](/docs/spinner/) says that something is at work, without promising a shape.
- [Progress](/docs/progress/) shows how much of a task is done.
- [EmptyState](/docs/empty-state/) is what to show once loading is over and there is nothing.

## Shapes made with classes

With no content to wrap yet, size each placeholder with classes: `h-4 w-2/5` for a line, `size-10 rounded-full` for an avatar. Mark the list `aria-busy` while it loads.

## Wrapping the real content

Put the content inside and flip `isLoaded`. While loading, the children are rendered but invisible, so the placeholder takes their exact size; once loaded, the Skeleton renders the children alone. `w-fit` keeps a line's placeholder as wide as its text.

</Example>

Wrap block elements, as here. Around an inline element, such as a Badge on its own, the placeholder takes the height of a line of text and can be a few pixels taller than the element.

## Styling

The placeholder is filled with `surface-3`, the same surface as a [Card](/docs/card/), and in the light theme that is lighter than the panels around it. Inside a card, or on a light panel, give it a tint that shows on any surface: the examples on this page pass `bg-default-bg`.
