## When to use

Pagination splits a long list into pages and moves between them: a commit history, search results, the rows of a table. It suits lists that people jump around in and come back to, since a page number is a place they can return to.

## Similar

- [Stepper](/docs/stepper/) leads through the steps of one task in order, each step with its own content; Pagination moves through more of the same.
- [Breadcrumbs](/docs/breadcrumbs/) lead up a hierarchy; Pagination moves along one level of it.

## Pages of a list

Keep the page in state and slice the list with it. Pages count from 1, and a `page` outside 1 to `total` is shown as the nearest one. With TanStack Table, pass `table.getPageCount()` as `total`, `pageIndex + 1` as `page`, and call `table.setPageIndex(page - 1)` from `onPageChange`.

## Narrow screens

`siblings` sets how many pages show on each side of the current one, and with it the number of slots. At the default size, seven slots and two arrows take about 380 pixels, more than a phone has once the page has its margins, and the buttons wrap onto a second line. `siblings={0}` keeps five slots: the first and last pages, the current one and two ellipses.

## Colours, sizes and disabled

`color` paints the current page, `primary` by default, so it follows the accent. `size` and `radius` take the Button's values, and `showControls={false}` drops the arrows. `isDisabled` dims every button and ignores the pointer, for instance while the next page loads.

## Accessibility

The control is a `<nav>` named "Pagination"; with more than one on a page, give each its own `aria-label`. The arrows are named "Previous page" and "Next page", and the ellipses are hidden from screen readers, so a reader hears only the pages there are buttons for.
