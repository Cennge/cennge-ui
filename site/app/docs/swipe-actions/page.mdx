## When to use

Use SwipeActions in lists people tidy often on a phone: a food diary, sets of a workout, habits, a list of runs. Put the action you take most on the leading side (done, re-run) and the destructive one on the trailing edge, where a full swipe reaches it, and pair a delete with an undo rather than a confirmation.

## Similar

- [Dropdown](/docs/dropdown/) is the menu each row already has; on its own it is enough for a desktop list.
- [ContextMenu](/docs/context-menu/) opens on right click and long press, for actions on any area rather than a row.

## Actions on both sides

`leadingActions` are revealed by a swipe to the right, `trailingActions` by a swipe to the left. Each has a `label`, an optional `icon` and `color`, and `onAction`. A swipe past half of the actions' width leaves them open; a tap on the row or anywhere else closes it again, without doing what the row does. Past three fifths of the row the action at the edge runs at once.

## The menu is the way in

A swipe is invisible until you know it is there. Every row keeps a button at its end, named "Actions for" its `label`, that lists all its actions in a menu. Hide it with `showMenu={false}` only when the row gives the same actions another way.

## Styling

The row slides over the actions, so it needs a background: `bg-surface-1` by default, the page's. Change it with `classNames.row` for a list on a card. A list with rounded corners and `overflow-hidden`, as on the plate, clips a focus ring that reaches past a row: draw the row's focus ring inside it (`focus-visible:-outline-offset-2`).
