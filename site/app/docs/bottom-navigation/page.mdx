## When to use

Use BottomNavigation for the few places a phone app is made of, those people switch between all day: Today, Log, Progress, Profile. Keep it to three to five, each a short label under an icon, and keep it on every top-level screen in the same order. On a wide screen give the same places a [Sidebar](/docs/sidebar/) instead, and hide the bar with a class such as `md:hidden`.

## Similar

- [Tabs](/docs/tabs/) switch between views inside one screen, at the top of it; BottomNavigation moves between the screens of the app.
- [Sidebar](/docs/sidebar/) holds the same places, and more of them, on a desktop.
- [BottomBar](/docs/bottom-bar/) is the bar for one screen's main action; it stands on top of the navigation when both are there.

## Links to pages

With `asChild`, an item renders your link, a Next.js `Link` here, and puts the icon and label inside it. Mark the current page with `isActive`, from `usePathname()` in a Next.js app.

## Fixed to the bottom

`position` is `fixed` by default: the bar pins to the bottom of the screen and pads itself for the home indicator, once your viewport meta has `viewport-fit=cover`. It writes its height to `--cennge-bottom-nav` on `<html>`, so a [BottomBar](/docs/bottom-bar/) and a [FloatingActionButton](/docs/floating-action-button/) sit above it by themselves, and your page can keep its last line clear:

```tsx
<main className="pb-[var(--cennge-bottom-nav,0px)]">…</main>
<BottomNavigation aria-label="Main" className="md:hidden">…</BottomNavigation>
```

`sticky` keeps it at the bottom of its scroll container instead, and `static` leaves it in the flow, as on this page.

## Accessibility

The bar is a `<nav>` named by its `aria-label`; each place is a button, or your link through `asChild`, named by its label and marked `aria-current="page"` when `isActive`. A count is part of the name, "Runs 3"; a dot is read as `badgeLabel`, "Profile new" unless you give it a word of your own. The icons are hidden from screen readers.
