## When to use

A Command palette is the fast way around an app for people who know what they want: open a repository, start a release, jump to settings, without a mouse. Put it in a `CommandDialog` that opens from anywhere with a shortcut, or place a `Command` in the page where a searchable list of actions belongs, like the plate above.

Keep everything in it reachable another way as well. A palette is quick for people who remember the words; menus and buttons show the rest what there is.

## Similar

- [Combobox](/docs/combobox/) picks a value for a form field and keeps it; a Command runs the item and forgets it.
- [Dropdown](/docs/dropdown/) lists a few actions behind a button with nothing to type; a Command searches many of them.

## A ⌘K palette

`CommandDialog` puts the Command in a dialog that opens and closes with ⌘K on a Mac and Ctrl+K elsewhere, and closes after an item runs. On this site ⌘K already opens the search, which is a CommandDialog as well, so this palette listens for ⌘J with `shortcut="mod+j"`; `shortcut={false}` turns the shortcut off. When the palette closes, focus goes back to where it was before it opened, whether a button or the shortcut opened it.

## Keywords and second lines

An item is found by its text and by its `keywords`: words people might type that are not in the name. When its children are not a plain string, give the text to search as `value`. Here “ssh” finds Deploy keys and “invite” finds Collaborators. `description` adds a second line, which is shown but not searched.

## When nothing matches

`emptyContent` replaces “No results”, and with the query controlled it can repeat what was typed. Screen readers hear how many items match after each change, as “2 results”; `labels.results` words that announcement, here in releases.

## Accessibility

The search field is a `combobox` that controls a `listbox`, and the active item is marked with `aria-activedescendant`, so focus never leaves the field while the arrows move. A group is named by its heading and hides itself while none of its items match. After each change to the query, a polite live region says how many items are left.

## Styling

`classNames` reaches the parts: `base`, `inputWrapper`, `input`, `list` and `empty`. The plate gives the list a `max-h-80`, so it scrolls past a few items; `className` styles the outer element, here with a border and `surface-3`.
